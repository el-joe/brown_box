Why Everyone Is Switching to Smart Home Devices This Year

Sarah Mitchell

July 25, 2026 · 6 min read

Five years ago, a smart home meant a single voice assistant answering trivia questions in the living room. Today it means lighting that adjusts to the sunset, thermostats that learn your schedule, and door locks you can check from the grocery store. The shift hasn't been sudden — it's been steady, and 2026 is the year it finally reached the mainstream.

Part of the change is price. Devices that cost triple digits a few years ago now start under $30, and most work together out of the box thanks to shared standards like Matter. But the bigger driver is simpler: people have realized that the right smart devices don't just add novelty, they remove small daily frictions.

What's Actually Worth Buying

Not every smart gadget earns a place on your counter. After testing dozens of devices across categories, a few clear winners kept coming up in every recommendation list — the ones that solve a real problem rather than just checking a "smart" box.

  • Smart plugs — the cheapest entry point, perfect for scheduling lamps and small appliances.
  • Video doorbells — consistently the highest-rated category for actual daily usefulness.
  • Robot vacuums — mapping technology has improved enough that these are no longer a gimmick.
  • Smart thermostats — often pay for themselves within a year through energy savings.
"The best smart home setup is the one you forget is 'smart' — it just quietly makes your day easier."

Start Small, Then Expand

The biggest mistake new adopters make is trying to automate everything at once. Start with one room and one clear problem — forgetting to turn off lights, or not knowing who's at the door — and build outward from there. A single well-chosen hub device can save you from a drawer full of incompatible apps.

Compatibility Still Matters

Before buying, check that a device supports the ecosystem you already use, whether that's a particular voice assistant or a phone platform. The Matter standard has made cross-brand compatibility far more reliable, but it isn't universal yet, so a five-minute check before checkout can save a return trip.

Smart home tech in 2026 isn't about living in the future — it's about spending less time on small, repetitive tasks. Whether that's one smart plug or a fully connected house, the return on a modest investment has never been clearer.
